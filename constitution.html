The Regen Commons Constitution

MVP constitution · version 2

The governing agreement of the commons: why it exists, how it is organized, and who holds which responsibilities. A short summary in plain words comes first, then the full text.

In plain words

This summary is not part of the constitution. Where the two differ, the full text below governs.

  • Why it exists: to care for the shared identity of the regenerative movement as a commons, protecting its integrity while keeping it open to many kinds of practice.
  • Freedom to act: anyone may act toward the commons’ purpose within the roles they hold, as long as they follow its protocols and uphold its values.
  • Who decides: an elected steward council manages the treasury and day-to-day governance by simple majority within agreed limits. All members form the Community Assembly, which advises on major decisions and selects stewards.
  • Members: people aligned with the purpose and values, admitted by invitation and consent. Members can vote on proposals within scope, propose projects and use member resources.
  • Projects: approved initiatives govern themselves within their agreed scope and receive funding as they reach milestones.
  • What is held in common: a governance forum, a shared treasury, brand assets, core intellectual property and the constitution itself.
  • Today: during the foundation period, the Membership Agreement sets out the parts in operation. Some parts, such as the treasury and on-chain roles, are scaffolding for later.

Context

Regen Commons is a cooperative governance container that stewards shared cultural, intellectual, and ecological assets for the regenerative movement during its MVP phase.

Freedom to participate

You are free to take any action that fulfills the Purpose of the organization and the Roles that you hold — as long as you do not violate any Protocols; and you seek to uphold the Values and principles of this Constitution.

Identity

The core meaning and intentionality of the Commons — why it exists, what it serves, and how it stays oriented over time.

Vision

The world we seek to help create

Regenerative culture, ecology, and economy flourish together as a coherent movement, safeguarded and sustained by shared governance infrastructure that keeps the Regen Movement open, trusted, and life-affirming.

Test: If this came true, the organization might no longer be needed.

Purpose

Why we exist now.

Regen Commons exists to care for regen identity as a shared cultural commons — protecting its integrity while enabling its plurality and cooperative use across the movement.

Test: This would remain true even if our form evolves.

Mission

What we do to fulfill our purpose.

Develop and maintain commons-based governance, recognition, and licensing systems that safeguard Regen Movement assets, support aligned use, and equip the ecosystem with knowledge, IP, and tools to thrive.

Test: It might change every few years — it’s how the purpose is pursued today.

Values

What we stand for.

Care
Grounding all work in responsibility to people, place, and planet.
Stewardship beyond ownership
Holding identity in trust, never as property.
Transparency
Creating clarity and openness in decisions and processes.
Reciprocity
Ensuring benefits flow back to those who nurture the commons.
Plurality
Honoring many expressions of regenerative practice while sustaining coherence.
Integrity
Prioritizing what is right for the commons over expedience or control.

Test: If this value was violated, the organization would feel out of integrity — even if its mission was achieved.

Worldview

A core worldview or system of understanding that informs how the organization relates to life, place, and purpose.

We understand the Regen movement as a living cultural commons. Legitimacy emerges through participatory process, identity and trust are stewarded (not owned), and living systems offer the deepest models for regenerative governance and cultural design. Shared assets must remain open, cared-for, and participatory through protective-yet-permeable membranes and adaptive protocols.

Test: If this philosophy were removed or violated, the organization’s identity and integrity would be fundamentally altered — even if its activities remained the same.

Mandates

A clearly defined area of responsibility the organization is committed to upholding through its roles, actions, and structures.

  • Steward Regen identity and brand integrity as a shared commons
  • Maintain transparent protocols for membership, funding, and brand/IP use
  • Curate and protect shared assets (knowledge, technology, branding, legal)
  • Enable contribution recognition and reciprocal value flows
  • Safeguard legitimacy via accountable governance and conflict transformation

Test: If this mandate were abandoned or neglected, the organization would no longer be fulfilling its reason for existing — even if other work continued.

Building blocks

The fundamental categories and building blocks we use to name, organize, and relate to ourselves. This section defines the core objects within the Commons’ operational reality.

Asset

This object defines an asset owned or managed by the Commons. Assets can be financial, digital, reputational, or intellectual property. Each asset is stewarded by one or more roles and governed by specific protocols.

Membrane

Boundaries that regulate participation, permissions, and trust thresholds. Membranes are semi-permeable boundaries that define coherent units of action, intention, and collaboration. Each membrane has entry/exit criteria, maintains appropriate internal governance, is accountable to values and protocols, and can steward its own subset of assets, roles, and responsibilities.

Protocol

The core life processes required to sustain and evolve the organism. This object defines a protocol, which is a repeatable, documented process for coordinating action within the Commons.

Role

Containers of agency and responsibility within the organization. This object defines a role within the Commons. Roles are distinct sets of responsibilities and permissions that can be held by members. They are the building blocks of the organization’s structure.

Notes for readers, not part of the constitution:

Multisig: a shared account that needs several stewards to approve before funds move. Gnosis Safe is one such account.

Hats role: an on-chain record of who holds a role. The constitution plans for these; they are not yet in use.
