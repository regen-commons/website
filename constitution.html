The Regen Commons Constitution

MVP constitution · text as of 11 November 2026

The governing agreement of the commons: why it exists, how it is organized, who holds which responsibilities and the protocols it follows. A short summary in plain words comes first, then the full text.

In plain words

This summary is not part of the constitution. Where the two differ, the full text below governs.

  • Why it exists: to give individuals and organizations common ground where they can pool resources, steward a shared identity and support regenerative action together.
  • Freedom to act: anyone may act toward the commons’ purpose within the roles they hold, as long as they follow its protocols and uphold its values and principles.
  • Joining: for the first six months, each seed member can invite up to five people. Invitees attend two or three onboarding events. If no member objects within seven days, the invitee can claim a membership credential; an objection goes to a consent vote of the assembly. Later, joining moves to a stricter process that members will design together.
  • Stewards: a council of five to seven stewards holds the shared treasury and carries out decisions. Seed members elect the first council; later stewards are to be chosen by sortition, a weighted random draw from nominees.
  • Decisions: each kind of decision has its own route. Role holders handle routine work, project funding goes to an advice process and a quadratic vote of members, and major allocations need collective consent, then wait one to three days before funds move. All members belong to the Commons Member Assembly, which meets monthly.
  • Conflict: handled in steps, from direct dialogue to mediation and, as a last resort, binding arbitration.
  • What is held in common: knowledge, technology, certification, branding and facilities, along with the constitution, the governance forum, the treasury, brand assets, core intellectual property and communication channels.
  • Today: during the foundation period, the Membership Agreement sets out the parts in operation. Some parts, such as the treasury and on-chain roles, are scaffolding for later.

Context

Regen Commons emerges in a moment when humanity faces intertwined ecological, social, and cultural crises that demand more than isolated solutions. While many individuals, organizations, and communities are already working toward regenerative futures, their efforts are often fragmented, under-resourced, or constrained by systems built on extraction and competition.

Regen Commons is a response to this need: a common ground where resources can be pooled and stewarded collectively in service of regenerative action. It is not itself the site of every action, but rather the enabling infrastructure — a cooperative field that supports diverse communities and activities to thrive by directing common pool resources toward their regenerative efforts.

This document — the organizational DNA and Constitution of Regen Commons — defines the identity, structure, and protocols that guide how Regen Commons operates. It articulates the shared understanding we hold, the roles we play, and the agreements we use to align around a life-centered ethos. In doing so, it provides coherence without control, ensuring that Regen Commons remains an open, evolving, and trustworthy foundation for self-organized collective stewardship.

Freedom To Participate

You are free to take any action that fulfills the Purpose of the organization and the Roles that you hold - as long as you do not violate any Protocols; and you uphold the Values and Principles of this Constitution.

Regenerative (“Regen”) identity describes a life-centered approach to organizing human and ecological systems in ways that restore, renew, and evolve the capacity of life to thrive in abundance across time.

It goes beyond sustainability (maintaining the status quo) toward reciprocity and flourishing: not just reducing harm, but actively repairing what has been degraded, strengthening resilience, and creating conditions for planetary well-being.

In practice, being regenerative means designing economies, cultures, and governance structures that produce dynamic equilibrium between human and natural systems through alignment with living-systems principles, and embed justice, equity, cohesion, belonging, trust, consent and interconnection at each level and scale of interaction.

Regen is both a worldview and a practice:

  • A worldview that shifts from extraction to reciprocity, from scarcity to abundance, from isolated fixes to whole-systems flourishing.
  • A practice of cultivating relationships, institutions, and infrastructures that center care for people, place, and planet.

Principles

Life-Centered Stewardship

Place the flourishing of life — human and more-than-human — at the center of decisions, designs, and actions.

Strong adherence
Prioritizing life-supporting outcomes in all decisions, even if it means reduced profit, slower timelines, or added complexity.
Weak adherence
Using environmental or social considerations as one of several factors, but still centering growth or efficiency.
Strong violation
Actively exploiting people or ecosystems for gain, disregarding well-being entirely.
Weak violation
Overlooking ecological or human impacts in minor decisions (e.g., using extractive suppliers without due diligence).

Whole-Systems Thinking

Work across ecological, social, cultural, and economic dimensions as interconnected parts of one living system.

Strong adherence
Consistently designing with feedback loops and interdependencies in mind, seeking holistic outcomes.
Weak adherence
Occasionally recognizing system linkages, but treating domains (e.g., finance, ecology) as mostly separate.
Strong violation
Designing deliberately siloed, linear, or extractive systems that cause cascading harms.
Weak violation
Ignoring cross-system effects (e.g., ecological harm hidden by financial gains).

Reciprocity & Renewal

Ensure that flows of value, energy, knowledge, and resources give back more than they take, fostering ongoing renewal rather than depletion.

Strong adherence
Embedding reciprocity into all exchanges, ensuring surplus for people, places, and ecosystems.
Weak adherence
Giving back occasionally (e.g., donations, offsets, energy), but not proportionally or systemically.
Strong violation
Extracting resources, labor, or culture without giving back, leaving lasting depletion.
Weak violation
Taking more than is given in small ways (e.g., underpaying contributors, neglecting replenishment).

Equity & Justice

Recognize and honor diverse ways of knowing, being, and belonging. Commit to fair and just participation in regenerative futures.

Strong adherence
Centering equity in governance, redistributing resources, and embedding justice structurally.
Weak adherence
Including marginalized voices occasionally, but without shifting power or resources.
Strong violation
Excluding, exploiting, or actively marginalizing vulnerable groups.
Weak violation
Overlooking equity in decisions, tokenizing participation.

Relational Wealth

Value trust, connection, and community as forms of wealth as essential as financial capital.

Strong adherence
Actively cultivating trust, reciprocity, and community bonds as the foundation of work.
Weak adherence
Naming relationships as important, but treating them as secondary to transactions.
Strong violation
Exploiting relationships for gain, sowing distrust, or eroding community fabric.
Weak violation
Breaking small commitments, neglecting community care.

Resilience & Adaptability

Design for flexibility, diversity, and adaptive capacity, ensuring continuity across generations.

Strong adherence
Building redundancy, diversity, and adaptive feedback loops into every system.
Weak adherence
Having contingency plans but not integrating resilience deeply into operations.
Strong violation
Creating rigid systems that collapse under stress, harming people or ecosystems.
Weak violation
Ignoring signals of change, relying on brittle or centralized solutions.

Movement Building

Act in ways that strengthen collective capacity — linking individuals, communities, and institutions into a regenerative movement greater than the sum of its parts.

Strong adherence
Sharing resources, knowledge, and infrastructure to grow collective capacity and alignment.
Weak adherence
Participating in networks but prioritizing individual visibility or outcomes.
Strong violation
Fragmenting movements through competition, co-optation, or extraction of legitimacy.
Weak violation
Withholding contributions, failing to acknowledge interdependence.

Participation & Co-Creation

Invite broad participation and distributed agency, trusting in collaborative intelligence to shape regenerative outcomes.

Strong adherence
Distributing decision-making power and trusting collective processes.
Weak adherence
Inviting participation but retaining central control over decisions.
Strong violation
Actively excluding stakeholders, enforcing top-down control, suppressing co-creation.
Weak violation
Limiting participation to surface-level input, ignoring community wisdom.

Abundance Mindset

Operate from the understanding that regeneration produces more possibility and vitality, not less, cultivating cultures of thriving rather than scarcity.

Strong adherence
Designing systems that unlock shared value, cultivating cultures of generosity and thriving.
Weak adherence
Using abundance language but still acting from fear of scarcity.
Strong violation
Operating from fear and zero-sum extraction, perpetuating scarcity culture.
Weak violation
Hoarding resources, failing to share knowledge or opportunity.

Purpose: Provide clear, lightweight pathways for how decisions are made across membranes, balancing human discernment with on‑chain enforcement.

Initialization: This protocol activates at MVP launch when roles are credentialed and the Governance Forum is open for proposals, records, and votes.

Participants: Members participate in advice/consent and voting; Stewards (multi-sig) execute approved actions on‑chain and manage treasury protocols; Project Leads coordinate project‑level execution.

Decision Domains & Flows
Membership Decisions

Foundation Period (Months 0-6) — Lower Barrier Entry:

  1. Invitation: Seed Members exercise individual authority to extend invitations (up to five per member) to prospective members demonstrating alignment with Purpose and Values
  2. Onboarding: Prospective members complete structured enculturation sequence of two to three (2-3) ritualized onboarding events facilitated by Gardeners and Stewards
  3. Lazy Consent Review: Upon completion of onboarding, prospective members enter seven (7) day lazy consent window
    • Any existing Member may raise substantive objections
    • Objections trigger formalized consent vote within Community Assembly
    • Absence of blocking objections results in automatic advancement
    • Batched processing permitted for operational efficiency
  4. Credential Claim: Successful candidates receive eligibility to claim non-transferable membership credential, constituting formal consent to Constitution and election to sortition pool
  5. Registry Update: EAS registry reflects new member status and governance eligibility

Transition Period (Month 6+) — Higher Barrier Entry: Following Foundation Period, upon Steward Council determination of adequate cultural coherence and governance capacity, membership admission transitions to enhanced verification model (specific mechanisms to be collaboratively defined during Foundation Period, potentially including attestation systems or Operations Team review).

Role Advancement: Advancement to higher‑responsibility roles (e.g. project lead, operator, gardener) requires advice process followed by consent at the appropriate membrane. Steward selection follows protocols defined in Steward Protocol, transitioning to sortition-based selection concurrent with membership barrier elevation.

Licensee Engagement

Generic Fair Use: Permissionless within published guidance and Commons conditional use license framework.

Certified Use: Steward-led consent process required for certified or narrow uses. Approved parties receive non-transferable access credential/NFT. Terms, duties, and royalty obligations are recorded in Governance Forum.

Intellectual Property Contribution

Submission: Contributors submit works with attribution and licensing intent to Governance Forum.

Review: Advice and peer review process conducted by community members and Gardeners.

Acceptance: Upon community acceptance, contributions become non-revocable commons resources with clear attribution, credentialed and licensed per IP Protocol.

Operational Decisions

Core Operations: Routine operational activities (credential issuance, infrastructure maintenance, software updates) are executed via delegated role-based authority with defined accountability cycles. Such delegations remain revocable via collective vote, with timelock enforcement where applicable.

Member Exit

Voluntary Departure: Members may exit voluntarily at any time. Upon exit, credentials are revoked and registry updated accordingly.

Inactive Member Review: Community conducts periodic review (every twelve months) to maintain registry accuracy. Inactive members may be removed following due process.

Non-Revocable Contributions: Accepted IP contributions and commons resources remain non-revocable regardless of member status.

Brand Resource Governance

Fair-Use Guidance: Stewards facilitate ongoing development and refinement of fair-use guidance, ensuring coherence between software licenses and brand licenses. Adoption of conditional use license framework for all IP associated with "Regen" designation.

Permissionless Use: Anyone may adopt Regen use patterns compliant with published fair-use guidelines. All members share responsibility for monitoring usage patterns and maintaining legitimacy.

Certified Use: Certified uses require formal consent process as outlined in Brand Usage Protocol.

Enforcement: Misuse follows Conflict & Tension Protocol escalation pathway. Persistent misuse may result in on-chain credential revocation.

Financial Decisions (Tiered Governance)

Operational Tier: Expenditures below defined financial and temporal thresholds (subject to twelve-month re-evaluation) are executed via role-based discretion (e.g., Treasurer Hat). Stewards execute directly via multi-signature authorization.

Project Tier: Project funding requests follow advice process, followed by quadratic voting among credentialed members. Successful votes authorize multi-signature execution per Treasury Management Protocol.

Strategic Tier: Major allocations require collective consent from Commons Member Assembly or Steward Council. Approved transactions execute via on-chain timelock (one to three day delay) to enable community review and emergency intervention if necessary.

Strategic Planning

Priority Setting: Annual strategic priorities established through collective sense-making processes within Community Assembly.

Action Alignment: Strategic initiatives require advice and consent processes to ensure collective alignment.

Automation: Only low-risk, routinized activities are candidates for on-chain automation.

Conflict Resolution

Escalation Pathway: Conflicts follow graduated resolution process: direct dialogue → facilitated mediation → binding arbitration.

Enforcement: Outcomes (including credential revocation, fund reallocation, or other remedies) are enforceable on-chain and recorded in Governance Forum.

Protocol Reference: See Conflict & Tension Protocol for detailed procedures and requirements.

Records & Transparency: Each decision should link to Governance Forum context, evidence of consent/votes, and, when relevant, transaction hashes or credential IDs. All meeting minutes are made available to members. Changelogs are maintained for Protocols and the Constitution.

Safeguards: Clear thresholds, timelocks for high‑stakes moves, revocability of delegated authority, and an active conflict protocol protect against capture and support legitimacy.

Process Flows Summary:

Membership (Foundation Period): Invitation → Onboarding Events (2-3) → Lazy Consent Review (7 days) → Credential Claim → Registry Update

Membership (Post-Foundation): Application → Enhanced Verification → Community Review → Approval → Onboarding → Credential Claim

Stewardship (Foundation Period): Nomination → Election by Seed Members → Term Confirmation → Ongoing Service

Stewardship (Post-Foundation): Nomination → Community Advice → Sortition Selection → Term Service

General Operations: Proposal → [Advice/Consent/Vote per Domain] → Execution → Documentation → Review

Purpose: Normalize conflict as a signal, provide a humane path to address tensions, and protect the commons’ integrity.

Purpose: This protocol governs how people become members, advance to deeper stewardship, and exit, while keeping records consistent on-chain. It establishes a phased approach to membership admission designed to ensure cultural coherence, collective trust, and operational readiness for distributed governance.

Initialization: The protocol becomes active upon adoption of the MVP Constitution and the publication of an initial whitelist of seed members.

Requirements: Alignment with Purpose and Values; demonstrated contribution and engagement; acceptance of Constitution and Protocols; completion of onboarding sequence and credential claim; willingness to participate in governance; maintenance of good standing. For project team participation, relevant skills and availability are required.

Participants: Seed Members initiate invitations; Gardeners and Stewards facilitate enculturation; Community Assembly exercises consent authority; prospective members complete onboarding requirements.

Membership Phases
Phase 1: Foundation Period (Months 0-6)

Lower Barrier Entry Process — Designed to establish a foundational membership cohort of approximately 100 individuals while maintaining cultural integrity and collective alignment.

Invitation Authority: Each Seed Member may extend invitations to up to five (5) individuals who demonstrate alignment with organizational Purpose and Values and show contributory capacity.

Onboarding Sequence: Prospective members must complete a structured enculturation process consisting of:

  1. Participation in two to three (2-3) ritualized onboarding events conducted over the Foundation Period
  2. Engagement with constitutional frameworks, decision-making protocols, and conflict transformation practices
  3. Integration into cultural norms and collective sense-making processes

Lazy Consent Review: Following completion of the onboarding sequence, each prospective member enters a seven (7) day lazy consent window during which:

  • Any existing Member may raise substantive objections based on alignment with Purpose, Values, or Protocols
  • Objections trigger a formalized consent process within the Community Assembly
  • Absence of blocking objections results in automatic advancement to credential claim eligibility
  • Batched processing of admissions may be employed for operational efficiency

Credential Issuance: Upon successful completion of the lazy consent period, prospective members receive eligibility to claim a non-transferable membership credential. Credential acceptance constitutes formal consent to this Constitution and includes election to join the sortition pool for future governance selection processes.

Registry Update: The EAS registry is updated to reflect new member status, participation history, and governance eligibility.

Phase 2: Transition Period (Month 6+)

Barrier Adjustment: Following the Foundation Period and upon determination by the Steward Council that sufficient cultural coherence and governance capacity have been established, membership criteria transition to a higher-threshold model:

Higher Barrier Entry Process — Designed to ensure sustained organizational integrity through enhanced verification mechanisms (specific model to be determined collaboratively during Foundation Period, potentially including attestation systems, Operations Team review, or other community-defined mechanisms).

Governance Democratization: Concurrent with membership barrier elevation, stewardship selection transitions to sortition-based processes, lowering barriers to governance participation while ensuring distributed authority.

Legacy Provisions
Seed Members
Automatically whitelisted from formation participants; claim role by accepting non-transferable credential within published claim window; EAS registry updated accordingly. Unclaimed credentials revert to standard admission pathways. Seed Member designation sunsets after twelve (12) months, transitioning to standard Member status.
Project Team Participation
Selection by Project Lead or via process defined in project scope; requires agreement to project-specific terms, timelines, and deliverables.

Advancement: Moving into roles uses an advice process to signal nomination + consent process at the relevant membrane level that the role contributes to, and is informed by transparent participation thresholds recorded in the registry.

Exit and List Hygiene: Members may depart voluntarily or via initiated exit. The community periodically reviews inactivity to keep the registry accurate. On exit, membership credentials are burned and the registry is updated. Access to assets is adjusted per protocols. Accepted IP contributions remain non‑revocable and part of the Common Pool Resources.

Key Responsibilities During Foundation Period

Seed Members: Exercise strategic discernment in cohort composition, extending invitations to individuals who demonstrate both alignment and contributory capacity. Responsible for cultivating a diverse yet coherent foundational membership body.

Gardeners and Stewards: Design and facilitate enculturation processes, onboarding sequences, and ritualized events. Ensure prospective members develop functional literacy in constitutional frameworks, decision-making protocols, and cultural practices. Prepare governance infrastructure and mechanisms to support transition to sortition-based selection in Phase 2.

Community Assembly: Exercise consent authority through lazy consent review processes. Maintain collective accountability for membership composition and cultural coherence.

Dependencies and Preparatory Work

Business Model Development: Establishment of clear membership benefits and value propositions (targeted for completion within initial months of Foundation Period) to inform invitation processes and support sustainable organizational operations.

Participatory Design: Engagement in collaborative business model design processes serves dual purposes: defining membership value propositions and identifying prospective members aligned with organizational direction.

Steps: Invitation → Onboarding Sequence (2-3 Events) → Lazy Consent Review (7 Days) → Credential Claim Eligibility → Registry Update → [Ongoing] Advancement or Exit

Initialization: The protocol becomes active when Regen Commons brand assets are utilized.

Participants: Licensed Partners and Members engage the brand; Stewards oversee guidance, approvals, and enforcement.

Generic Fair Use: Anyone may use the Regen Commons brand within the published fair‑use guidance and the Commons conditional use license, without a bespoke approval. Community members help observe and uphold the guidance.

Monitoring & Enforcement: Misuse triggers the conflict pathway first (dialogue and mediation). If unresolved, stewards may revoke membership on‑chain. Stewards periodically update guidance and criteria with community input.

Steps: Generic Fair Use (permissionless): anyone may adopt Regen use patterns that comply with published fair-use guidelines and the Commons conditional use license; Certified Regen (consented): steward-led consent process for certified/narrow uses; values alignment assessment; appeals available; Credentialing: issue non-transferable brand access credential; record terms; royalties logged where relevant; Monitoring & Enforcement: transparent oversight; conflict pathway first; revoke credential if misuse persists; Governance: stewards support cyclical revisions to fair-use guidance and certified criteria; members may propose changes

associatedRoles
Steward (Multisig), Project Lead

Purpose: This protocol establishes clear, tiered pathways for making and executing financial decisions from the Commons treasury.

Initialization: The protocol activates when the treasury multisig is configured and stewards are credentialed.

Participants: Stewards lead treasury execution; Project Leads request releases after milestone verification; members participate in mid‑ and high‑tier approvals.

Tiers & Approvals: Operational expenditures under a defined threshold are handled through delegated role‑based discretion. Mid‑tier allocations use an advice process followed by a quadratic vote by credentialed members; approved votes authorize the multisig to execute. Strategic allocations require collective consent (assembly/council) and execute via on‑chain timelock.

Transparency & Records: All allocations, votes, and transactions are published with sufficient detail to enable auditability and community oversight.

Steps: Ops Tier (< threshold): role-based discretion (e.g., Treasurer Hat); direct multisig execution; Project-Tier: advice + quadratic vote by credentialed members; multisig executes approved tx; Strategic Tier: collective consent (assembly/council) for major allocations; timelocked execution; Transparency: publish allocations and reports; maintain on-chain records.

Purpose: Establish legitimate stewardship selection through phased governance approach: initial elected Steward Council during Foundation Period to ensure organizational stability and cultural coherence, transitioning to sortition-based selection to prevent power concentration while ensuring competency and distributed authority.

Initialization: Change in role state proposed, either through nomination period or electoral process as appropriate to current governance phase.

Requirements: Active Member in good standing; availability for meetings and transaction signing when necessary; demonstrable trust and process/treasury competency; commitment to transparency and Conflict Protocol; capacity to facilitate governance infrastructure development and member enculturation.

Participants: Seed Members conduct Foundation Period election; Community Assembly provides nominations and advice in subsequent phases; eligible Members may be nominated or self-nominate; selected Stewards receive credentials and permissions.

Selection Phases
Phase 1: Foundation Period (Months 0-6, Minimum)

Higher Barrier Steward Selection — Designed to ensure organizational stability, treasury security, and governance capacity development during critical formation phase.

Initial Election: Seed Members nominate and elect Interim Steward Council (five to seven members) for minimum six-month term via democratic voting mechanism (e.g., Snapshot or equivalent platform). This establishes initial legitimacy and accountability while foundational membership cohort is being established.

Term Duration: Interim Steward Council serves minimum six-month term, extendable by Seed Member determination until such time as:

  1. Foundation Period objectives are substantially achieved
  2. Membership cohort demonstrates adequate cultural coherence and governance literacy
  3. Infrastructure for sortition-based selection is operationally ready

Foundation Period Responsibilities: Interim Steward Council bears enhanced responsibility for:

  • Designing and supporting member enculturation processes and ritualized onboarding events
  • Developing governance agreements and decision-making infrastructure
  • Preparing sortition mechanisms and selection criteria
  • Ensuring foundational members develop functional literacy in constitutional frameworks
  • Stewarding treasury with heightened accountability during formation phase
  • Facilitating participatory business model development
Phase 2: Transition to Distributed Governance (Month 6+)

Lower Barrier Steward Selection — Concurrent with elevation of membership admission barriers, stewardship selection barriers are lowered to democratize governance participation.

Nomination Process: Following Foundation Period, peer nomination process opens where eligible Members may be nominated or self-nominate based on demonstrated participation, competency signals, and alignment with Purpose and Values.

Community Advice: Community Assembly provides advice on nominations, selection criteria, and governance needs during open advice period.

Sortition-Based Selection: Random selection from nominated pool employs weighted criteria including:

  • Participation history and contribution patterns recorded in registry
  • Trust signals and peer attestations
  • Relevant expertise and demonstrated competencies
  • Commitment to term obligations

This approach prevents popularity contests and power consolidation while ensuring distributed authority and ongoing competency.

Role Assignment: Selected Stewards receive multi-signature permissions (Gnosis Safe signer access) and Hats credentials with appropriate scope and authority for treasury management and governance execution.

Term Management: Post-Foundation Period Stewards serve defined terms (typically nine months) with staggered rotation to ensure continuity. Terms may be renewed through sortition process. Early removal requires two-thirds (2/3) vote of Members. Regular accountability cycles maintain quality and legitimacy.

Transition Protocol: Outgoing Stewards transfer knowledge, context, and permissions to incoming cohort through structured handoff processes, ensuring smooth governance continuity and institutional memory preservation.

Steps:

Foundation Period: Nomination → Candidate Statements → Election by Seed Members → Term Confirmation → Role Assignment → [Ongoing Service] → Transition or Extension Determination

Post-Foundation: Nomination Period → Community Advice → Sortition Selection → Credential Issuance → Term Service → Renewal or Transition

Participants: Project Leads, Project Participants, and Stewards execute this protocol; the Community Assembly contributes advice and context.

Notes for readers, not part of the constitution:

Lazy consent: a proposal goes ahead unless someone objects within a set time.

Quadratic vote: each extra vote a member puts on the same option costs more than the last, so strong preferences count without letting a few voices dominate.

Sortition: choosing people by a random draw from a pool. The constitution plans to weight the draw by participation, trust signals and expertise.

Multisig: a shared account that needs several stewards to approve before funds move. Gnosis Safe is one such account.

Timelock: a set wait between approving a transaction and carrying it out, which leaves time to review or stop it.

Hats role: an on-chain record of who holds a role. The constitution plans for these; they are not yet in use.

EAS registry: a list of members and their status kept with the Ethereum Attestation Service, which the constitution plans to use.
