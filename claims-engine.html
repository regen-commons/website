Claims Engine

Trusted land stewardship claims, made with local AI.

Land stewards are paid only when their care is verified and trusted. Our AI-assisted workflow, used in soil-carbon reviews, matches evidence to requirements while people decide. We will move it from commercial models to local ones that communities own and govern, so stewards can make claims offline and keep their evidence.

Last updated 20 November 2026

Why it matters

When a steward reports that soil carbon rose or trees were planted, someone must check the evidence against the rules that claim must meet. That check stands between caring for land and being trusted and paid for it.

It is slow, specialist work that usually sends a community’s records to a distant reviewer. For most smallholders and community groups it is out of reach, so their stewardship goes unrecognized and unpaid.

Where it stands

  1. In use today

    Regen’s registry team uses the AI-assisted review workflow in live reviews of soil-carbon projects. It sorts a project’s documents, matches passages to each requirement, and drafts a finding that shows its sources. A human reviewer accepts, corrects or rejects every match.

    Pilot communities record regenerative work in Green Goods, where the community reviews every submission and nobody approves their own work.

  2. Being built

    The Claims Engine, which will record each claim, its evidence, and every machine and human judgment, so anyone can verify the result.

    Small local models, one for each review task.

  3. Not yet

    Stewards do not make claims themselves.

    Small models are tested offline but not used in live reviews.

How it works

This is the planned path, in four steps.

  1. 1

    Capture

    On a phone

    A steward records the work. No connection is needed.

  2. 2

    Draft

    A model proposes

    On a laptop, a small local model drafts the claim in the browser and shows every value beside the words it came from. A person accepts or corrects each one.

  3. 3

    Review

    Someone else decides

    Someone else in the community approves or rejects it. Review never needs AI.

  4. 4

    Share

    The community chooses

    The community chooses when a claim goes to a reviewer or funder.

    Nothing leaves the device unapproved.

The Claims Engine record

Each claim, its evidence, and every machine and human judgment, so anyone can verify the result.

The smallest model that passes

For each task we start with no model at all. Document sorting and requirement matching already work that way. We step up to a small model only where a comparison with human reviewers shows it is needed.

People’s corrections will train the models, and only with approval that names training as the purpose.

What stays with the community

Collected

Green Goods rounds locations to about 110 metres, strips location data from photos, and records consent with each report. Each field records whether a person or a model produced it.

Stored

Today, approved work becomes a permanent public record. The local path we are building keeps work on its author’s device until the author and the community approve its release.

Used

Reviewers’ decisions train the models. A community’s evidence is used only if it opts in.

Governed

Communities will choose what they record and release. Regen Commons’ steward council governs the engine’s terms.

Who is building it

Regen Commons brings together the teams behind two open projects: Regen Network, which builds the Claims Engine, and Green Goods, an independent open-source stewardship app.

SecondMuse is prototyping a dashboard of outcome claims, built on the Claims Engine’s schema, for a cacao landscape in Central Sulawesi, Indonesia.
